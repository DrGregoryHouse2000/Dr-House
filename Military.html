Military

Disclaimer: make sure you have a strong economy before you need to build up a strong and dominant Military

Economic & Budget Commitment:

Sustained defense spending funds equipment, maintenance, and readiness without straining the broader economy. A 2026 Ipsos survey for the Halifax International Security Forum notes strong public backing for national defense, though governments often face public skepticism over actually delivering equipment on time

Technology & Domestic Industry:

Investing in research, modern hardware, cyber defense, and domestic manufacturing reduces reliance on foreign supply chains and keeps forces competitive

Personnel & Training:

Recruiting, retaining, and rigorously training active and reserve forces—supported by strong leadership and healthy demographics—turns equipment into real capability.

Logistics & Readiness:

Maintaining spare parts, fuel, transport, and medical support ensures units can deploy and sustain operations rather than sitting idle.

Threat-Aligned Strategy & Alliances:

Tailoring forces to geography and realistic threats—such as territorial defense, regional power, or global reach—and partnering with allies multiplies reach and deterrence.

What to do when In war!?

Production:

Sustaining a war requires adequate financial resources, industrial production, and logistical support. Wealthier nations or those with resource windfalls can fund more equipment, training, and infrastructure, and endure prolonged conflicts. Industrial capacity ensures the supply of weapons, fuel, and other necessities.

Strategic and Tactical Effectiveness:

Military strategies must target enemy weaknesses and avoid overcommitting to strengths This includes:

  • Intelligence and reconnaissance to know the enemy’s plans

  • Flexibility in tactics and operations.

  • Diplomacy and alliances to isolate the opponent and gain support

Technology and Innovation

Access to modern weapons, communications, and technology can give a decisive edge. Innovations in tactics, logistics, and intelligence can also multiply the effectiveness of existing forces.

Will to Fight and National Resolve

RAND and military historians stress that the “will to fight” is the single most important factor in war .This is the nation’s determination to continue fighting and achieve victory, which translates into morale, cohesion, and the willingness of soldiers and civilians to sustain the conflict. Without strong national resolve, even the best-equipped forces can collapse under pressure.
